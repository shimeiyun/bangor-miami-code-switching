Defining a switch.

Do participant-level switching rates differ across the corpus's ordinal education-code groups? The comparison depends on a consistent definition of what counts as a switch.

HOW I COUNTED

Two decisions shape the analysis.

First, I linked a speaker to questionnaire metadata only when recording, age and sex identified one record. Ambiguous cases stayed unresolved. Second, I counted adjacent eng → spa or spa → eng labels only within the same recording, speaker and utterance. A mixed label breaks the sequence.

The education groups are my recoding of ordinal corpus codes, not confirmed qualification categories. The comparison is exploratory and cannot establish a causal effect.

GROUP DETAILS

Low = codes 1–2; Middle = 3; High = 4–5. These are researcher-defined groups, not official education categories. The Low group has five participants.

WHAT THIS DOES AND DOES NOT SHOW

The measurement is useful; the group effect is uncertain.

The strict matching rule left fewer speakers for analysis, but avoided assigning education data to uncertain identities. Under the chosen switch definition, there is no reliable evidence of a difference between education-code groups. The measure misses other kinds of code-switching, and the analysis does not account for conversation topic or interlocutors.
